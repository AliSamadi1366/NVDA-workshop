Lesson 4: Molecular Geometry — VSEPR Theory

Predicting 3D molecular shapes from electron-domain repulsion

The Core Idea: Electron Domains Repel

VSEPR (Valence Shell Electron Pair Repulsion) theory predicts molecular geometry by assuming that electron domains — bonding pairs and lone pairs — arrange themselves as far apart as possible to minimize repulsion.

  • Bonding domain: A region where a pair of electrons is shared between two atoms (single, double, or triple bond each count as one domain).
  • Lone pair domain: A nonbonding electron pair on the central atom.
Key rule: Count electron domains (not bonds!) around the central atom. The domain geometry determines the approximate bond angles. The molecular geometry (shape) is the domain geometry with lone pairs "invisible."

Lone Pairs Compress Bond Angles

Lone pairs occupy more space than bonding pairs because they are attracted to only one nucleus. This stronger repulsion pushes bonding pairs closer together:

CH₄ (0 lone pairs)  →  109.5°
NH₃ (1 lone pair)   →  ~107°
H₂O (2 lone pairs)  →  ~104.5°

In trigonal bipyramidal geometries, lone pairs always occupy equatorial positions (120° apart) to minimize 90° repulsions.

Worked Examples

Example 1: CO₂

Central atom: C. Valence electrons: 4. Two double bonds to O = 2 domains. No lone pairs on C.

Domains: 2 → Linear, 180°.

Example 2: NH₃

Central atom: N. Valence electrons: 5. Three single bonds to H (3 domains) + 1 lone pair = 4 domains.

Domain geometry: Tetrahedral. Molecular geometry: Trigonal pyramidal. Angle: ~107°.

Example 3: SF₄

Central atom: S. Valence electrons: 6. Four single bonds to F (4 domains) + 1 lone pair = 5 domains.

Domain geometry: Trigonal bipyramidal. Lone pair goes equatorial. Molecular geometry: See-saw.

Polarity from Geometry

Molecular geometry determines whether bond dipoles cancel:

  • Symmetrical shapes (linear, trigonal planar, tetrahedral, trigonal bipyramidal, octahedral) with identical substituents → nonpolar (e.g., CO₂, CCl₄, SF₆).
  • Asymmetrical shapes or symmetrical shapes with different substituents → polar (e.g., H₂O, NH₃, CH₃Cl).

Step-by-Step VSEPR Procedure

  1. Draw the Lewis structure; identify the central atom.
  2. Count electron domains on the central atom (bonds + lone pairs).
  3. Determine the electron-domain geometry from the domain count.
  4. Place lone pairs to minimize repulsion (equatorial in trigonal bipyramidal).
  5. Name the molecular geometry (ignore lone pairs).
  6. Predict approximate bond angles.
