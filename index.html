Chemistry Workshop: Buffers & Molecular Geometry

What you will learn

  • Why a mixture of a weak acid and its conjugate base resists pH changes when acid or base is added
  • How to calculate the pH of a buffer using the Henderson–Hasselbalch equation
  • What determines how much acid or base a buffer can absorb before its pH shifts significantly
  • How to predict 3D molecular shapes using VSEPR theory (electron-domain repulsion)

Why buffers matter

Buffers are everywhere in chemistry and biology. Your blood maintains a pH of 7.4 thanks to the carbonic acid/bicarbonate buffer system. Many biochemical reactions, industrial processes, and analytical methods depend on buffers to keep pH stable. Understanding buffers is a foundational skill for any chemist.

Prerequisites

You should be comfortable with:

  • Acids, bases, and the pH scale
  • Weak acid dissociation and Ka
  • Conjugate acid–base pairs
  • Basic algebra and logarithms
